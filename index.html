Research

I work at the intersection of artificial intelligence with research and development and with manufacturing. I am Digitalization Manager at BASF, an Adjunct Professor in Chemical Engineering and a Visiting Scientist in Biological Sciences at LSU.

I have spent 25+ years turning data into tools that people use: in industry at BASF, in academic genomics at Penn State and LSU, and at a software startup. My background combines biology, engineering and computer science, and I have used all three throughout my career.

I design and build bioinformatics and industrial data systems, from requirements through deployment, and I choose open-source software when it fits. I mentor development teams on best practice and lead cross-functional teams.

AI meets evolutionary biology

I build pipelines for evolutionary biology and apply them to gene families across the tree of life. For each family, I collect the proteins from many species, build the family tree, reconstruct the ancestral sequences, and test which sites and which branches show positive selection or relaxed constraint. I then place those changes on predicted protein structures to ask whether they fall in regions that matter for function. For example, I have studied gene families in cancer signaling, cellular protein quality control, lipid metabolism, plant development, chromatin and various non-coding families.

With colleagues in Biological Sciences at LSU, we used RNA-seq in D. melanogaster and C. elegans to study differentially expressed genes under different biological conditions. We built pipelines for enriched gene sets (KEGG, GO) and for co-expressed gene networks, and we looked for conserved non-coding regulatory elements in those networks. This work contributed to two 2025 studies: lysosome regulation in C. elegans and a short-sleep mutation in Drosophila.

AI meets manufacturing

With colleagues in Chemical Engineering at LSU and at BASF, we use machine learning to model chemical production. We predict quality and production variables that cannot be measured directly (soft sensors), detect anomalies in equipment sensor data, build dynamic dashboards for quality and production data, and use unsupervised clustering to approximate manufacturing operating conditions. We recently described a graph-temporal surrogate architecture for multivariate process modeling.

Earlier work

As a bioinformatics scientist on NSF-funded Plant Genome Projects at Penn State (2001-2009), I developed bioinformatics pipelines and visualization tools for genome annotation and analysis, and I helped annotate and publish the poplar and papaya genomes. I built the PlantTribes gene-family database, which we used to publish gene-family papers on the MADS-box and APETALA2-like transcription factors, and I worked on early genome duplications and early diversification in flowering plants.
